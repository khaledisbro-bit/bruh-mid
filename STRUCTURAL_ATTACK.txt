STRUCTURAL_ATTACK.TXT - recovering the key with nothing from outside the file

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

The key for this sample was recovered by known plaintext: words guessed at the
string table's shut entries, confirmed by the file's own arithmetic and then by its
tag. That works once. It does not transfer to another sample, because another
sample has different words.

This is the attack that does transfer. It is NOT finished. What follows is what it
has established and what is still open, so the next pass starts from facts.

WHAT THE FILE GIVES WITH NO KEY

The real slice deserialises completely without a key. Four functions, every
instruction, and every constant's SHAPE:

  19 packed constants, each one {68, blob, seed, 1, 1}
  the blob lengths: 3, 3, 3, 23, 23, 23, 23, 23, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24,
                    25, 25, 25, 25, 25
  the seeds, in the open: 1089, 2066, 3043, 4020, 4997, then 1929084 upward,
                          then 876013735 and 876014712
  the seeds of the first five step by exactly 977, and so do the next ones

The seed is the ONLY thing the key material varies with. So for two constants of
the same shape, the three numbers their key is made of differ by a fixed multiple
of the seed difference: 31, 313 and 257 times it. That is a relation between
ciphertexts with no unknowns but the key itself.

WHAT THAT ALREADY PINS

Taking fourteen constants at once and requiring their first accumulator to follow
its own step relation:

  the first plaintext byte is 174, which the file's reader calls a table
  the second is 4, so that table's array part holds four entries
  the third is 7
  the first accumulator is down to 64 readings from 2^32

All three bytes are the same across all fourteen, each agreeing fourteen ways. The
fourth byte is not shared, because that is where the constants start to differ from
each other, and the relation alone leaves 64 readings of the high end.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN

  the third byte came out 7, and 7 is not one of the reader's tags - 134, 222,
  202, 15, 161 and 174 are. So either the array count is encoded differently than
  assumed, or the shape of these constants is not the shape the first slice used.
  That is the next thing to settle, and it settles from the first slice, which is
  open and whose constants can be read byte for byte.

  the second and third accumulators gave no reading at all under the same
  assumption, which is the same problem showing up louder: their bytes are not
  shared across constants of different length.

  the three byte constants are the way in. A three byte value can only be a one
  character number, a one character string, or an empty table - three shapes, a
  hundred or so readings in total - and all three of them have known seeds. That
  pins the low bytes of the first accumulator exactly, with no assumption about
  what a longer constant looks like.

WHAT WOULD FINISH IT

The first slice is fully open and uses the same reader, the same shapes and the
same arithmetic. Every assumption this attack needs can be checked there first,
against constants whose plaintext is already known. Then the same check runs on
the shut slice with nothing guessed.

Until that is done this is not a general tool, and it is not claimed to be.
